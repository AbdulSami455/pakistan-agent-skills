## Juvenile Justice System Act 2018 — Key Features

- Defines a "child" for JJSA purposes generally as a person who has not attained a specified age (commonly cited as under 18 years) at the time of the alleged offence — verify the exact statutory age definition and whether any offence-category or borderline-age provisions create exceptions.
- Establishes Juvenile Courts (or designates existing courts to exercise juvenile jurisdiction) with a mandate to try child-accused cases separately from adult proceedings; prohibits joint trial of a child with an adult co-accused in the same proceeding as a general rule.
- Introduces a general presumption favoring bail for a child accused, and restricts pre-trial detention, with statutory preference for alternatives such as remand to a borstal institution, probation-officer supervision, or release to a parent/guardian rather than ordinary adult detention facilities — the presumption is not absolute and is understood to admit exceptions for the most serious offence categories; hedge specific offence-category carve-outs rather than asserting bail is unconditional in all cases.
- Requires the appointment and involvement of probation officers and, in some formulations, legal-aid/counsel provision for the child accused throughout the process.
- Provides for diversion — resolving certain less serious matters through alternative mechanisms (e.g., reconciliation between parties, community-service-type resolutions) instead of formal trial — intended to reduce unnecessary formal criminal processing of children for minor offences.
- Prohibits the death penalty and, per the general thrust of juvenile-justice reform in this area, restricts corporal/certain punishment forms for offences committed as a child, consistent with Pakistan's CRC obligations — verify exact current statutory punishment restrictions rather than asserting a specific provision from memory.

## Child Marriage — Minimum Age and Provincial Variation

- The Child Marriage Restraint Act, 1929 (as amended and continued post-partition) historically set differing minimum marriage ages for males and females (commonly cited historically as 18 for males and 16 for females under the Act's original/longstanding terms), with penalties for those who solemnize, contract, or facilitate an underage marriage. Treat this age figure as needing verification against the current amended text applicable outside Sindh, since reform bills (including federal-level proposals to raise the female minimum age to 18) have been introduced/debated at various points without necessarily being uniformly enacted nationwide.
- Sindh enacted the Sindh Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2013, which raised the minimum marriage age to 18 years for both males and females within Sindh — this is a materially higher and gender-equalized threshold compared to the 1929 Act's historical terms, and represents Sindh's exercise of its post-18th-Amendment provincial legislative competence over the subject.
- Other provinces (Punjab, KP, Balochistan) have, at various points, considered but not necessarily uniformly enacted an equivalent 18-year, gender-equal threshold; do not assume any one of these provinces currently applies the same standard as Sindh without verification.
- A marriage solemnized in violation of the applicable minimum-age law is generally treated as attracting criminal penalties against those responsible for solemnizing/facilitating it, though the marriage's civil validity under religious personal law and its criminal-law invalidity/penalization under the restraint act are analytically distinct questions that have been the subject of ongoing legal and policy debate in Pakistan; avoid asserting a single settled position on civil validity without hedging.

## Zainab Alert, Response and Recovery Act 2020

- Enacted following widely publicized child abduction and murder cases (including the case that gave the Act its name), the Act establishes a formal rapid-alert and coordinated-response mechanism for missing, abducted, or trafficked children.
- Establishes (or designates) a dedicated response agency/mechanism — commonly referenced as the Zainab Alert, Response and Recovery Agency (ZARRA) — tasked with receiving reports, triggering a rapid public/institutional alert, and coordinating search-and-recovery efforts with police and other relevant authorities.
- The Act's alert mechanism is conceptually comparable to international "Amber Alert"-style rapid-notification systems, adapted to Pakistan's institutional and media landscape, though the specific operational scope (e.g., which provinces/regions have full operational rollout, integration with police FIR processes) should be verified rather than assumed uniformly implemented nationwide.
- The Act's mechanism supplements, and does not replace, the underlying criminal investigation into the abduction/trafficking offense, which proceeds through ordinary FIR registration and police investigation (or specialized units, depending on the case) under general criminal procedure.

## National Commission on the Rights of Child Act 2017

- Establishes the National Commission on the Rights of Child (NCRC) as an independent statutory body.
- Mandate includes: monitoring implementation of child-rights law and policy across Pakistan (including in light of Pakistan's obligations under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child); reviewing existing and proposed legislation for consistency with child-rights standards; receiving and inquiring into complaints alleging violations of children's rights; and making recommendations to government for legal and policy reform.
- NCRC is a monitoring, advisory, and complaints-inquiry body — it does not itself function as a court, prosecutor, or police agency, and an NCRC inquiry finding is not equivalent to a criminal conviction or judicial determination; matters requiring criminal investigation/prosecution are referred to or pursued through the ordinary criminal-justice or juvenile-justice system as applicable.
- Provincial-level child-rights commissions/bodies have, in some provinces, been separately established or discussed, distinct from the federal-level NCRC and from provincial child protection bureaus (which handle welfare casework rather than rights-monitoring).

## Provincial Child Protection Institutions (Brief Cross-Reference)

- Separate from JJSA (offender-focused) and NCRC (monitoring/advisory), several provinces have established child protection bureaus/authorities handling child-welfare casework — e.g., removing a child from an abusive or exploitative situation, arranging institutional or foster-type care, and coordinating with courts on child-welfare orders.
- This casework layer is institutionally and functionally distinct from both juvenile-justice procedure and NCRC's rights-monitoring role; verify the current provincial-specific institutional name and structure before citing.

## Extraction Notes

- Always identify which of the four statutes (JJSA 2018, marriage-restraint law, Zainab Alert Act 2020, NCRC Act 2017) a question concerns before answering, since they serve distinct protective functions.
- Always differentiate Sindh's 18-year minimum marriage age from the position in other provinces when answering a marriage-age question; do not give one blended national figure.
- Describe JJSA as modifying general CrPC procedure for a child accused, not as a wholly separate criminal code.
- Describe NCRC's role as monitoring/advisory/complaints-review, not prosecutorial.
- Describe the Zainab Alert Act's mechanism as supplementing, not replacing, ordinary FIR/police-investigation processes.
- Flag specific age thresholds, penalty provisions, and provincial institutional names not confidently known as "(verify)".
