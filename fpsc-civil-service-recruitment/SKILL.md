# FPSC and CSS Civil Service Recruitment

## Overview

Use this skill for Pakistan's premier federal civil-service recruitment pipeline: the Federal Public Service Commission (FPSC) as the constitutional/statutory recruiting body for federal posts, and the Central Superior Services (CSS) competitive examination through which most federal occupational groups are staffed. This is a recruitment-institution and exam-structure skill, not a general test-administration skill.

## Coverage

- **Federal Public Service Commission (FPSC)**: a constitutional body (Article 242 of the Constitution provides for public service commissions) operationalized in its current federal form by the Federal Public Service Commission Ordinance, 1977, responsible for recruitment to federal civil service posts through competitive examination and other prescribed selection processes, and for advising the federal government on civil-service personnel matters as required. FPSC conducts the CSS examination annually along with numerous other federal recruitment tests (e.g., for various BPS-17 and other federal posts advertised outside the CSS cadre).
- **CSS competitive examination structure**: the CSS exam is the principal annual entry route into Pakistan's senior federal occupational groups, structured around a written examination comprising **compulsory subjects** (commonly including English Essay, English (Precis and Composition), General Science & Ability, Current Affairs, Pakistan Affairs, and Islamic Studies/Comparative Religions for non-Muslim candidates — exact current paper list and marks distribution should be verified against the current FPSC CSS syllabus for the relevant examination year, since paper structure, compulsory-subject list, and total marks have been revised more than once, including a significant restructuring in recent years) and **optional subjects** chosen by the candidate from an approved list spanning subject clusters (e.g., group I business/accounting, group II governance/political science/international relations, group III history, group IV literature/languages, group V science/mathematics, group VI law) — candidates typically select a fixed number of optional papers from these groups subject to FPSC's rules on permissible combinations. A qualifying written stage is followed by a psychological assessment/psychometric evaluation and a viva voce (interview) before final merit-list allocation to occupational groups.
- **Occupational groups (allocation)**: successful candidates are allocated to specific federal occupational groups based on merit rank and preference, commonly including the **Pakistan Administration Service (PAS)**, **Police Service of Pakistan (PSP)**, **Foreign Service of Pakistan (FSP)**, **Pakistan Customs Service**, **Inland Revenue Service (IRS)**, **Office Management Group (OMG)**, and several other specialized groups (e.g., Postal Group, Railways, Information Group, Commerce & Trade Group, Secretariat Group) — the exact current list, group names, and merit-based allocation logic should be checked against the current Civil Servants (Composition, Cadre and Seniority) rules or FPSC/Establishment Division notification in force, since group names and structure have been adjusted periodically (e.g., historical group renaming and restructuring exercises).
- **Age and eligibility criteria**: CSS eligibility generally requires a minimum age (commonly 21) and a maximum age (commonly around 30, with standard relaxations for certain categories such as government servants, and additional prescribed relaxations for other specified categories) at the time of application, along with a minimum educational qualification (typically at least a bachelor's degree, with specific group-based educational-qualification requirements for some technical groups) — exact current age limits, relaxation categories, and educational-qualification detail should be verified against the current year's FPSC CSS advertisement/rules, since these are set by rules that can be revised.
- **Provincial equivalents**: each province operates its own public service commission for provincial civil-service recruitment, structurally analogous to FPSC but operating under separate provincial legislation and separate examination/cadre structures: the **Punjab Public Service Commission (PPSC)**, **Sindh Public Service Commission (SPSC)**, **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Public Service Commission (KPPSC)**, and **Balochistan Public Service Commission (BPSC)**. These bodies recruit for provincial-government posts (e.g., Provincial Management Service equivalents) and are institutionally and legally separate from FPSC — a CSS pass does not confer eligibility for a provincial civil-service post, and vice versa.

## Use This Skill For

- explaining FPSC's constitutional/statutory basis and its role as the federal recruiting body
- walking through the CSS exam pipeline: compulsory + optional written papers, qualifying threshold, psychological assessment, interview, merit-based group allocation
- identifying major federal occupational groups (PAS, PSP, FSP, Customs, Inland Revenue, and others) and clarifying that allocation is merit-and-preference based, not a direct application to a specific group
- explaining general CSS age and educational-eligibility criteria at a structural level while flagging exact current limits for verification
- distinguishing FPSC/CSS (federal recruitment) from provincial public service commissions (PPSC, SPSC, KPPSC, BPSC) and their separate provincial cadres
- clarifying that FPSC also conducts numerous non-CSS federal recruitment tests, not only the annual CSS exam

## When Not to Use This Skill

- For NTS's National Aptitude Test (NAT), Graduate Assessment Test (GAT), or NTS-administered job/recruitment testing services, use `nts-testing-service` instead — NTS is a separate testing body with a different mandate (it administers tests for many organizations on a contracted basis, including some public-sector recruitment, but it is not FPSC and does not administer the CSS examination).
- For HEC university admission or higher-education-specific testing/accreditation matters, use `hec-*` skills as appropriate.
- For general civil-service pay-scale (BPS) structure or pension/service-rule questions unrelated to recruitment, this skill does not cover those in depth; treat as out of scope unless directly tied to CSS eligibility or group allocation.

## Routing Rules

- If the question is about the federal CSS exam, FPSC's recruitment mandate, or federal occupational-group allocation, apply this skill.
- If the question is about NTS-administered tests (NAT, GAT, or contracted recruitment tests for other organizations), route to `nts-testing-service` — do not describe NTS as administering the CSS exam, since FPSC administers CSS directly, not NTS.
- If the question is about a provincial civil-service exam or a provincial-government post, route to the relevant provincial public service commission (PPSC/SPSC/KPPSC/BPSC) framing and clarify it is legally separate from FPSC.
- If the question needs a precise current age limit, exact paper-wise marks distribution, or current group-wise vacancy numbers, flag for verification against the current FPSC CSS advertisement/syllabus rather than asserting a fixed figure from memory.

## Extraction Workflow

1. Confirm whether the question concerns federal (FPSC/CSS) or provincial (PPSC/SPSC/KPPSC/BPSC) recruitment — these are separate legal and institutional tracks.
2. If federal, confirm whether the question is about the CSS exam specifically or another FPSC-administered federal recruitment test, since FPSC's mandate extends beyond CSS.
3. For CSS structure questions, separate the compulsory-subject paper list from the optional-subject group list, and note the sequential stages (written exam, qualifying threshold, psychological assessment, interview, merit list, group allocation).
4. For occupational-group questions, clarify that allocation follows merit rank and candidate preference rather than direct application to a named group.
5. For eligibility questions, state age/education criteria at a general structural level and flag exact current figures (age limits, relaxations, qualification specifics) as needing verification against the current year's official CSS advertisement/rules.
6. If a provincial exam is referenced, name the correct provincial commission and note it operates under separate provincial legislation with its own cadre structure, not interchangeable with FPSC/CSS.

## Technical Rules

- FPSC (federal) and the provincial public service commissions (PPSC, SPSC, KPPSC, BPSC) are legally and institutionally separate bodies under separate legislation; a CSS pass does not confer eligibility for a provincial civil-service post, and provincial exam success does not confer eligibility for federal CSS-cadre posts.
- FPSC administers the CSS examination directly; do not attribute CSS administration to NTS, which is a separate testing organization operating under a different, broader (multi-client) testing mandate.
- CSS occupational-group allocation is merit-and-preference based following the combined written/psychological-assessment/interview process — candidates do not apply directly to a single named group at the outset.
- Do not state a precise, current CSS age limit, relaxation rule, marks distribution, or paper list as settled fact without flagging it for verification against the current year's official FPSC CSS syllabus/advertisement, since these rules are revised periodically.
- FPSC's mandate covers many federal recruitment tests beyond CSS; do not conflate "FPSC test" generically with "CSS exam" specifically.

## Validation Checklist

- Confirm whether the question is about federal (FPSC/CSS) or provincial commission recruitment before answering.
- Confirm NTS is not credited with administering the CSS exam.
- Confirm occupational-group allocation is described as merit-and-preference based, not direct application.
- Flag any specific current age limit, marks distribution, or paper list as needing verification against the current official syllabus.
- Distinguish CSS specifically from FPSC's broader federal-recruitment testing mandate when relevant.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing FPSC with NTS, or describing NTS as administering the CSS examination.
- Treating PPSC/SPSC/KPPSC/BPSC as regional branches of FPSC rather than separate provincial legal bodies.
- Describing CSS group allocation as a direct application choice rather than a merit-and-preference-based outcome after the full selection process.
- Citing a precise, unhedged current age limit or paper-wise marks breakdown without flagging it for verification, given that CSS rules are periodically revised.
- Omitting the psychological assessment/interview stages and describing CSS selection as based on the written exam alone.
