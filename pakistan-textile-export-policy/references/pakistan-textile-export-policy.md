## Textile Policy Framework

- Pakistan has periodically issued dedicated Textile Policy documents (historically under a Ministry of Textile Industry, later merged into the Ministry of Commerce/Ministry of Commerce and Textile) setting multi-year targets for export growth, value-addition (shifting export composition from cotton yarn and greige/grey cloth toward higher-value garments, made-ups, and knitwear), technology upgradation (e.g., support schemes for machinery modernization), skills development, and compliance with international buyer standards.
- Policy instruments historically bundled under a Textile Policy have included duty drawback/rebate schemes, mark-up rate support/subsidized financing for exporters (e.g., long-term financing facilities administered through SBP for textile machinery), and the energy-tariff relief discussed below.
- Because Textile Policy documents are issued for specific multi-year windows and are not always renewed on schedule, the current operative status of any given policy should be checked against the Ministry of Commerce's latest notifications rather than assumed continuous.

## Energy Tariff / Regional Competitiveness Support

- A recurring feature of Pakistan's textile-export competitiveness policy has been a concessional/capped electricity and RLNG tariff for export-oriented industry (zero-rated sectors, historically framed as the "five zero-rated export sectors": textile, leather, carpets, surgical goods, and sports goods), intended to narrow the energy-cost gap with regional competitors such as Bangladesh, India, and Vietnam.
- This concessional regime has a documented history of being introduced, withdrawn (e.g., amid IMF program conditionality concerns about broad-based energy subsidies), and periodically reinstated in some modified form; industry bodies (e.g., APTMA) have repeatedly lobbied for its restoration or extension when withdrawn.
- Any specific tariff rate (PKR/kWh for electricity, or price per MMBTU for RLNG) attributed to export industry should be treated as a point-in-time figure requiring verification against the current SRO/notification (verify current figure).

## Export Facilitation Scheme (EFS)

- EFS is administered by FBR under customs law and consolidated several predecessor duty/tax-exemption schemes for exporters (including earlier manufacturing-bond, export-oriented-unit, and temporary-importation schemes) into a unified framework.
- Mechanics: EFS allows registered manufacturer-cum-exporters to import raw materials, intermediate goods, and (in later scheme iterations) plant/machinery without upfront payment of customs duty, sales tax, and other applicable levies, subject to furnishing a bank guarantee/insurance guarantee or indemnity bond, adherence to a notified input-output coefficient ratio for the finished export product, and utilization/export-realization reporting within prescribed timelines.
- Textile and apparel manufacturer-exporters are among the largest users of EFS given the sector's heavy reliance on imported cotton (in years of domestic shortfall), man-made fibers, dyes/chemicals, and specialized machinery.
- EFS eligibility, input-output ratios, and compliance/reporting requirements are periodically revised by FBR through SROs; specific current ratios or procedural deadlines should be verified against the latest FBR notification rather than assumed static.

## GSP+ Status with the EU

- The EU's Generalised Scheme of Preferences Plus (GSP+) grants qualifying developing countries preferential (largely duty-free) access to the EU market across a wide tariff-line coverage, conditional on ratification and effective implementation of 27 international conventions covering human rights, labour rights, environmental protection, and good governance.
- Pakistan has been a GSP+ beneficiary since 2014 (initial award), and textiles/apparel constitute by far the largest category of Pakistani exports benefiting from GSP+ preferential access to the EU market.
- The EU conducts periodic monitoring/review cycles of beneficiary countries' compliance with GSP+ conditionality, and the scheme itself is subject to periodic EU-level renewal/recast (the GSP+ regulation has been renewed/extended more than once since Pakistan's original award). The specific status of the current review cycle, any compliance concerns flagged by the EU, and the regulation's current expiry/renewal timeline should be verified against current European Commission GSP+ documentation rather than stated definitively (verify current review cycle and status).

## TDAP's Textile-Sector Role

- TDAP, under the Ministry of Commerce, undertakes textile-specific export promotion: subsidizing participation in international textile and apparel trade fairs, organizing buyer-seller matchmaking events, publishing sector-specific market-access guidance, and providing input into national export strategy formulation for the textile sector.
- TDAP's role is promotional/facilitative; it does not set customs duty/tax incentives (an FBR function) or the overarching Textile Policy (a Ministry of Commerce policy-setting function).
