## Board of Revenue Structure

- Each province maintains its own Board of Revenue as the apex provincial body for land administration, revenue records, and related fiscal functions tied to land. While institutional details vary by province, the traditional field hierarchy below the BoR has historically included tiers such as **Patwari** (village-level record-keeper), **Tehsildar/Naib Tehsildar** (tehsil-level revenue officer), and **Assistant Commissioner (Revenue)/Deputy Commissioner** (district-level revenue authority), though digitization reforms have altered some of these roles' day-to-day function (e.g., shifting record issuance from manual patwari registers to computerized service centers).
- The Deputy Commissioner's office (or its revenue-department equivalent) has historically also been the source of the **DC rate** — the provincially notified valuation used as the base for stamp duty calculation — separate from FBR's own federal valuation tables.

## Land Record Digitization

- **Punjab**: The Punjab Land Records Authority (PLRA), established to modernize and computerize land records, operates the Arazi Record Management Information System (ARMIS) and associated Land Record Management Information System (LRMIS) infrastructure, allowing computerized Fard (record-of-rights) issuance through Arazi Record Centers, replacing manual patwari-register lookups for many transactions. This is the most extensively documented provincial digitization program.
- **Other provinces**: Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan have pursued their own land-record computerization efforts with different institutional names, timelines, and coverage levels; do not assume PLRA/ARMIS branding or rollout maturity applies to other provinces. Verify the current program name and district-level coverage status for any province other than Punjab before citing specifics.

## Agricultural Income Tax

- Agricultural income is excluded from the federal tax base under the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, because taxation of agricultural income is a **provincial** subject under Pakistan's constitutional division of taxing powers (reflected in the Fourth Schedule/Federal Legislative List treatment of agricultural income).
- Each province levies agricultural income tax under its own statute (e.g., Punjab's Agricultural Income Tax Act, 1997, as amended, and analogous statutes in Sindh, KP, and Balochistan), typically administered through the provincial Board of Revenue rather than a separate tax-collection agency, often using a combination of acreage-based and income-based assessment methods depending on land holding size and cropping.
- Agricultural income tax collection has historically represented a small share of provincial own-source revenue relative to the sector's share of GDP, a point frequently raised in fiscal-reform and IMF program discussions about broadening Pakistan's tax base — treat any specific current collection figure as needing verification rather than assumed static, and avoid implying uniform enforcement intensity across provinces.

## Stamp Duty

- Provincial stamp duty is charged under provincially adapted Stamp Act legislation (tracing back to the Stamp Act, 1899) on instruments effecting property transfer (sale deed, gift deed, exchange deed, etc.), calculated as a percentage of the transaction value — with the **DC rate** (or a provincially notified valuation table) typically used as the minimum valuation base rather than the parties' stated price alone, similar in structural purpose to (but administratively separate from) FBR's own federal property valuation tables.
- Provincial governments have periodically revised stamp duty rates and/or introduced online stamp-paper/e-stamping systems in some provinces; treat specific current rates as needing verification.
- Registration of the transfer instrument itself proceeds under the Registration Act, 1908 framework via the district Sub-Registrar's office, which in most provinces sits within or closely alongside the Board of Revenue's district administrative structure.

## Extraction Notes

- Always specify which province's Board of Revenue, agricultural income tax statute, or digitization program is being discussed; do not generalize Punjab-specific detail (especially PLRA/ARMIS) to other provinces without verification.
- Keep provincial DC rate/stamp duty valuation clearly separate from FBR's federal notified property valuation tables (see `fbr-property-valuation`) — they serve different taxes administered by different levels of government.
- Keep agricultural income tax (provincial fiscal levy) separate from Ushr (Zakat Ordinance religious levy administered through Zakat Councils) even though both apply to agricultural land/produce.
- Flag any specific current tax rate, digitization coverage percentage, or collection figure as needing verification rather than asserting a fixed number.
