## Legal and Institutional Structure

- The Zakat and Ushr Ordinance, 1980 created a compulsory, state-administered Zakat deduction system layered on top of — not replacing — the individual religious obligation. It applies specifically to enumerated "assets" (mainly certain bank/financial-institution-held instruments), not to a person's total wealth.
- **Council hierarchy** (structure and nomenclature per the Ordinance, subject to amendments over time and provincial administrative evolution post-18th Amendment):
  - **Central Zakat Council** — apex body, sets overall policy, allocation formulas, and oversees the Central Zakat Fund.
  - **Provincial Zakat Councils** — oversee provincial administration and allocation to District Zakat Committees.
  - **District/Local Zakat Committees** — the front-line body historically responsible for identifying mustahiqeen (eligible recipients) in a locality and disbursing guzara allowances and other support directly.
- **Deducting agents**: Banks, National Savings Centres, and other specified financial institutions holding Zakat-able accounts/instruments act as statutory deducting agents — they are legally obligated to deduct on the valuation date (first day of Ramazan) and remit to the Central Zakat Fund, not merely to advise account holders to pay independently.

## Deduction Mechanics

- **Covered instruments** typically include savings bank accounts, Profit and Loss Sharing (PLS) accounts, and specified National Savings Schemes certificates/accounts held by Muslim citizens, when the balance is at or above the notified nisab on the valuation date.
- **Valuation date**: first day of Ramazan each Hijri year — deduction is a point-in-time snapshot, not a running monthly deduction.
- **Nisab**: statutorily pegged to the value of a specified quantity of silver (historically referenced around 612.32 grams of silver under the Ordinance's original formulation); because silver's rupee value fluctuates, the nisab rupee figure is redetermined and notified before each Ramazan — always treat a specific rupee nisab figure as needing current-year verification (verify current figure) rather than citing an old number as still applicable.
- **Exemption declaration**: Account holders who are non-Muslim, who follow a fiqh that does not accept this deduction mechanism (commonly cited: certain Shia jurisprudence positions), or who wish to self-assess can file a declaration with the deducting institution (commonly referenced administratively as a "CZ-50" form or successor form) in advance of the valuation date to avoid compulsory deduction.

## Ushr

- Ushr is levied under the same Ordinance on the produce of agricultural land owned/held by a Muslim, above a specified minimum produce threshold (nisab-equivalent for Ushr, expressed in agricultural produce terms), intended to parallel the classical Islamic ushr/kharaj concept.
- Unlike bank-account Zakat, Ushr collection was never comprehensively automated at a national institutional level — assessment relied more heavily on local Zakat Committees and provincial revenue/land-record machinery, and actual collection intensity has varied significantly by province and over time. Treat any claim of comprehensive, uniform Ushr collection nationally with skepticism; the practical administrative reality has been much patchier than the Zakat deduction-at-source system.
- Ushr under this Ordinance is a religious levy tied to produce, distinct from provincial agricultural income tax, which is a separate statutory tax on agricultural income assessed by provincial Boards of Revenue.

## Central Zakat Fund — Disbursement Heads

- Guzara (subsistence) allowances to widows, orphans, the disabled, and the poor.
- Educational stipends/scholarships for needy students.
- Health-related assistance (e.g., support toward medical treatment for the deserving poor).
- Support to deeni madaris (religious seminaries) and other permissible categories specified under the Ordinance and subsequent policy guidelines.
- Disbursement flows from the Central Zakat Fund down through Provincial Councils to District/Local Zakat Committees, which handle final identification and payment to individual mustahiqeen.

## Extraction Notes

- Never state a current nisab or Ushr threshold figure as a fixed fact; flag it as periodically revised and requiring current-year verification.
- Keep the compulsory statutory deduction system distinct from an individual's independent zakat calculation and payment, which is a matter of personal religious practice outside this Ordinance's administrative machinery.
- Do not conflate the Central Zakat Fund with BISP or any other federal cash-transfer program — they have entirely separate legal bases, funding sources, and targeting mechanisms.
- Note that post-18th Amendment provincial administrative roles in Zakat/Ushr administration have evolved; verify current federal-vs-provincial division of a specific administrative function rather than assuming the original 1980 federal structure remains unchanged in all respects.
