## Institutional Layers

| Layer | Instrument | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Bilateral coordination | Joint Cooperation Committee (JCC) + Joint Working Groups (JWGs) | Apex Pakistan-China coordination on CPEC direction, sector-specific working-group oversight |
| Domestic CPEC coordination | CPEC Authority Act, 2022 | Statutory basis for the CPEC Authority's role coordinating CPEC project implementation across federal ministries, provinces, and private sector, and liaising with Chinese counterparts |
| Domestic SEZ legal mechanism | Special Economic Zones Act, 2012 | General legal framework for declaring and regulating SEZs (federal/provincial), independent of CPEC specifically |

## Joint Cooperation Committee (JCC)

The JCC is the apex bilateral body overseeing CPEC, co-chaired by senior Pakistani and Chinese officials (on the Pakistani side, historically led at the level of the Ministry of Planning, Development & Special Initiatives). It is supported by sector-specific Joint Working Groups covering areas including energy, transport infrastructure, Gwadar, industrial cooperation/SEZs, agriculture, and socioeconomic development, which develop and review project-level proposals ahead of JCC review.

## CPEC Authority Act, 2022

Places the CPEC Authority — originally constituted through executive/ordinance action — on statutory footing via an Act of Parliament. Its mandate centers on coordination, planning, and facilitation of CPEC project implementation across federal ministries, provincial governments, and the private sector, and serving as a central interface with Chinese project counterparts. The precise institutional history (ordinance-to-Act transition dates, any subsequent restructuring or reporting-line changes) should be verified against the current Gazette text, as CPEC institutional arrangements have been revisited more than once since CPEC's initial launch.

## Special Economic Zones Act, 2012

- Enables federal and provincial governments to jointly declare an area a Special Economic Zone.
- Establishes SEZ Authority structures (federal-level and provincial-level boards/authorities) responsible for zone development approval, developer licensing, and enterprise approval within a declared zone.
- Empowers "one-window facilitation" — a single regulatory interface intended to consolidate approvals that would otherwise require separate applications to multiple federal and provincial agencies.
- Applies as a general domestic legal tool for SEZs, not exclusively to CPEC-linked zones, though CPEC industrial cooperation has been a major driver of SEZ policy attention and specific zone designations since the mid-2010s.

## SEZ Fiscal Incentives (General Structure)

- Typically include customs-duty and tax exemptions on capital goods/machinery imported for zone development (developer level) or for setting up operations within the zone (enterprise level).
- Typically include an income-tax holiday/exemption period for developers and/or zone enterprises for an initial specified period.
- Exact current exemption periods, eligible categories, and any phase-out or rollback should be verified against the current Income Tax Ordinance provisions and SEZ Act rules in force, since SEZ fiscal incentives have been subject to periodic review, phase-out debate, and scrutiny linked to IMF programme conditionality on tax-expenditure reduction.

## Commonly Cited CPEC-Linked SEZs

- **Rashakai Special Economic Zone** — Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, along the M-1 motorway corridor near Nowshera/Mardan; frequently cited as an "early harvest" priority SEZ under CPEC industrial cooperation.
- **Dhabeji Special Economic Zone** — Sindh, near Thatta/Karachi; another frequently cited priority zone.
- **Allama Iqbal Industrial City** — Faisalabad, Punjab; cited as a CPEC-linked industrial zone/SEZ.

Current development and operational status (infrastructure completion, occupancy, actual enterprise relocation) for any of these zones should be verified against current reporting, since these projects have experienced delays and phased progress relative to original announcement timelines.

## Extraction Notes

- Keep the three institutional layers (JCC bilateral coordination, CPEC Authority domestic coordination, SEZ Act domestic legal mechanism) analytically distinct.
- Do not describe the CPEC Authority as executing projects directly; its statutory role is coordination and facilitation.
- Do not treat every Pakistani SEZ as a CPEC project, or treat CPEC as limited to SEZs — CPEC also covers energy and transport infrastructure outside the SEZ legal category.
- Flag exact current SEZ fiscal-incentive rates/periods and any named SEZ's current operational status as needing verification rather than restating early-announcement figures as current fact.
