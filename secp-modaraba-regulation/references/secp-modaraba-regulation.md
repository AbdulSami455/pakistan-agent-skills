## Legal and Structural Framework

- Pakistan's Modaraba sector was created through the Modaraba Companies and Modaraba (Floatation and Control) Ordinance, 1980, one of the earliest pieces of Islamic-finance-specific legislation in Pakistan, predating the modern Islamic banking framework by roughly two decades.
- A Modaraba operates on the classical mudarabah contract principle: one party (the Modaraba Management Company, acting as **mudarib**, the managing/working partner) manages a business using capital contributed by certificate holders (acting collectively as **rabb-ul-maal**, the capital-providing partner), with profits shared per an agreed ratio disclosed in the prospectus and losses (absent management negligence/misconduct) borne by the capital providers.
- **Modaraba Management Company (MMC)**: Must itself be incorporated as a public limited company under the Companies Act, 2017 and separately registered/certified by SECP as fit to manage a Modaraba. The MMC earns a management fee and/or a share of profit as compensation for managing the Modaraba's business; it does not own the Modaraba's assets or certificate-holder capital in its own right.
- **The Modaraba**: A separate certificated entity floated to the public (or placed privately, subject to rules) through issuance of Modaraba certificates representing units of ownership/participation in the Modaraba's business and profit/loss, distinct from the MMC's own share capital.

## Regulatory and Shariah Oversight Bodies

- **SECP** — the statutory regulator for Modaraba registration, floatation (prospectus) approval, ongoing compliance/reporting, and enforcement, exercised through SECP's Specialized Companies Division (the division historically responsible for NBFCs, Modarabas, and other specialized regulated entities) — distinct from SECP's Company Law Division, which handles ordinary company incorporation under the Companies Act, 2017.
- **Religious Board for Modarabas** — a statutory religious/Shariah supervisory body constituted under the 1980 Ordinance specifically for the Modaraba sector, tasked with certifying that Modaraba business activities and modes of financing comply with Islamic Shariah principles. This is structurally and institutionally separate from: (a) individual Islamic banks' in-house Shariah Boards regulated by SBP, and (b) Shariah Advisory Boards used for Islamic mutual funds or Sukuk structuring — each operates under its own sector-specific legal framework even though all draw on similar Shariah finance principles.
- **Modaraba Association of Pakistan (MAP)** — the industry association representing Modaraba Management Companies collectively; performs sector advocacy, coordination, and information-sharing functions but has no statutory regulatory authority — do not present MAP statements as SECP regulatory determinations.

## Floatation, Listing, and Certificate Mechanics

- A Modaraba is "floated" through public issuance of Modaraba certificates per an SECP-approved prospectus, specifying the Modaraba's permissible business activities, profit-sharing ratio between the MMC and certificate holders, and management fee structure.
- Modaraba certificates historically have been listed and traded on the Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX), giving certificate holders a secondary-market exit mechanism — structurally similar to a closed-end fund's exchange-traded shares, and unlike an open-end mutual fund's NAV-based redemption directly from the fund.
- A Modaraba's permissible business scope (e.g., leasing/ijarah-based financing, trading, participation financing, or a combination) is fixed at floatation per its prospectus and reviewed for ongoing Shariah compliance; it is not an open-ended general commercial mandate the way an ordinary company's objects clause can be broad.

## Distinctions From Adjacent Structures

- **Vs. mutual funds**: Mutual funds (open-end or closed-end) are regulated under the NBFC Rules/Regulations, managed by an Asset Management Company (AMC), and (for open-end funds) redeemed at NAV directly with the fund — a fundamentally different capital-raising and redemption mechanism from a Modaraba's certificate structure. See `mufap-mutual-funds`.
- **Vs. REITs**: Real Estate Investment Trusts are a separate SECP-regulated trust structure under REIT-specific regulations, focused on real-estate asset pooling, distinct from a Modaraba's broader permissible business scope and mudarabah-contract legal basis.
- **Vs. ordinary companies**: A Modaraba is not itself registered as a company under the Companies Act, 2017 — only the MMC (its manager) is. The Modaraba itself is a certificated vehicle created and governed by the 1980 Ordinance.

## Extraction Notes

- Always keep the MMC (manager, itself a Companies Act company) and the Modaraba (the managed certificated fund) as two distinct entities when describing structure, ownership, or regulatory touchpoints.
- Name the Religious Board for Modarabas specifically when discussing Shariah oversight in this sector; do not substitute a banking-sector or mutual-fund Shariah body.
- Treat the Modaraba Association of Pakistan as an industry association, not a source of binding regulatory determinations.
- Flag any current count of active Modarabas, aggregate sector assets, or specific certificate pricing as needing current verification rather than citing remembered figures.
