## PMEX Background and Corporate Structure

Pakistan Mercantile Exchange Limited (PMEX), originally incorporated as the National Commodity Exchange Limited (NCEL) in 2002, was renamed Pakistan Mercantile Exchange and began live futures trading in 2007. It is a demutualized, corporatized exchange — meaning trading/membership rights are separated from exchange ownership, similar in structural philosophy to PSX's own 2016 demutualization, though PMEX and PSX are wholly separate corporate entities with separate shareholders, separate boards, and separate product lines. PMEX is the **only** SECP-licensed commodity futures exchange in Pakistan; there is no competing licensed commodity futures exchange as of this writing.

PMEX's shareholders have historically included financial institutions and, at various points, strategic/technology partners; the exact current shareholding structure should be verified against PMEX's own disclosures rather than assumed static, since exchange ownership can change through share transfers over time.

## Legal and Regulatory Framework

- **Futures Market Act, 2016**: the primary statute governing futures exchanges, futures brokers, futures contracts, and clearing houses in Pakistan. It replaced the earlier legal framework that had governed commodity exchanges (which traced back to Provincial-era commodity exchange laws and subsequent corporatization directives) with a unified, SECP-administered federal statute specific to futures markets.
- **SECP's role**: SECP licenses and regulates PMEX as a futures exchange, licenses futures brokers (analogous to, but separately licensed from, PSX-facing stockbrokers), approves new futures contract specifications before they are listed, and exercises inspection, investigation, and enforcement powers over the futures market under the Act — mirroring, in a parallel statutory track, its capital-markets oversight role under the Securities Act, 2015 for PSX.
- **Distinction from the Securities Act, 2015**: the Securities Act, 2015 (and related rules) governs the securities market — PSX, stockbrokers, listed-company securities, and market intermediaries dealing in "securities" as defined for equities/debt instruments. Futures contracts on PMEX are a distinct instrument class regulated under the separate Futures Market Act, 2016 framework, even though SECP is the common apex regulator for both markets.

## Contract Types Traded on PMEX

PMEX has, over its operating history, listed and periodically revised futures contracts across several broad categories (the live, currently-tradable contract roster should always be confirmed against PMEX's current contract specifications, since PMEX lists and delists products over time):

- **Precious metals futures**: gold and silver futures contracts, denominated and settled in PKR, generally cash-settled (though some contract variants have offered physical delivery options) — used both for hedging and speculative price exposure to international bullion price movements as transmitted into the local market.
- **Currency futures**: PKR-denominated futures on currency pairs (most notably USD/PKR), allowing hedging of exchange-rate exposure through an on-exchange, centrally cleared instrument rather than an over-the-counter forward.
- **Agricultural commodity futures**: contracts linked to locally relevant agricultural commodities (cotton and cotton-related contracts have historically been a focus given Pakistan's cotton/textile sector, alongside contracts on other crops introduced at various points) — intended to give growers, ginners, and textile-sector participants a domestic price-hedging instrument.
- **Index and other financial futures**: PMEX has at various points introduced or explored index-linked and other financial futures products beyond metals/currency/agriculture; the current active list should be checked against PMEX's own product disclosures.

## Clearing, Margining, and Settlement

- PMEX operates a clearing-house function for futures contracts traded on its platform, requiring registered clearing members to post **initial margin** when opening a futures position and to meet **variation/maintenance margin** calls as positions are marked to market daily against settlement prices.
- This margin-based, leveraged structure is fundamentally different from PSX's cash-market equity settlement, which uses a T+2 delivery-versus-payment cycle without daily mark-to-market margining on ordinary cash purchases.
- Futures contracts carry defined **expiry/delivery cycles** (e.g., monthly contract months), after which open positions are either cash-settled against a reference settlement price or, for contracts offering physical delivery, proceed to a delivery process — the specific mechanics vary by contract and should be confirmed against that contract's specification sheet.

## Membership and Brokerage

Trading access to PMEX is through PMEX-registered Trading/Clearing members (futures brokers), a SECP-supervised registration category distinct from PSX's stockbroker (TREC holder) registration. Some brokerage groups hold both PMEX futures-broker registration and PSX stockbroker registration under separate corporate or divisional structures, but the two registrations, capital-adequacy requirements, and conduct rules are administered as separate regimes under their respective statutes.

## Extraction Notes

- Always distinguish PMEX (Futures Market Act, 2016) from PSX (Securities Act, 2015) as separate exchanges, separate statutes, and separate membership bases, even where SECP is the shared apex regulator.
- Do not state a specific current margin rate, contract lot size, tick size, or expiry calendar without flagging it as needing verification against PMEX's current contract specifications, since these are revised by exchange circular from time to time.
- When describing agricultural commodity futures, note that the specific crops with active contracts have changed over PMEX's operating history; do not assume a fixed permanent list.
- Treat any claim about PMEX's current shareholding structure as needing verification against PMEX's own current disclosures.
