# Workers Welfare Fund and Profit Participation Reference

## Source

- Workers' Welfare Fund Ordinance, 1971 (as amended) — governing statute for WWF.
- Companies Profits (Workers' Participation) Act, 1968 (as amended) — governing statute for WPPF.
- Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) guidance on WWF treatment within income-tax assessment: `https://www.fbr.gov.pk`
- Provincial labour department notifications where WWF/WPPF administration has been devolved post-18th Amendment (administration and thresholds can vary by province; verify current provincial framework before citing a specific rate).

## Workers' Welfare Fund (WWF)

- Established under the Workers' Welfare Fund Ordinance, 1971. WWF is a statutory levy on the income of industrial establishments, used to fund worker welfare measures (e.g., housing schemes, education, and other welfare projects administered through the Fund).
- Historically computed as a percentage of an establishment's taxable/total income above a specified threshold, assessed alongside the entity's income-tax return; the levy is collected by FBR at the federal level even though the underlying welfare-spending mandate is worker-welfare-oriented.
- Applicability thresholds and the specific percentage rate have been amended over time via Finance Acts — do not cite a specific rate or income threshold as current without checking the latest Finance Act/FBR circular, since this is exactly the kind of figure Finance Acts routinely revise.

## Workers' Profit Participation Fund (WPPF)

- Established under the Companies Profits (Workers' Participation) Act, 1968. WPPF requires eligible companies to allocate a percentage of annual profits to a fund for the benefit of workers, distinct from ordinary wages or bonuses.
- The allocation is profit-linked (calculated on the company's annual accounting profit for the year, per the Act's definition), not a flat per-employee or payroll-period deduction — this is the key mechanical distinction from WWF, which is assessed against taxable income at tax-return time, and from ordinary payroll deductions, which recur monthly regardless of profitability.
- Funds are typically distributed to workers directly or invested for their benefit per the Act's mechanics and associated rules; specific distribution mechanics and eligibility (e.g., which categories of employees qualify as "workers" under the Act) should be confirmed against the Act's current text and any amending Ordinances.

## Key Distinctions

| Feature | WWF | WPPF |
|---|---|---|
| Governing statute | Workers' Welfare Fund Ordinance, 1971 | Companies Profits (Workers' Participation) Act, 1968 |
| Basis of calculation | Percentage of taxable/total income above a threshold | Percentage of annual accounting profit |
| Assessed alongside | Income-tax return (FBR) | Annual profit appropriation / financial close |
| Purpose | Funds worker housing/welfare projects via a central Fund | Direct profit-sharing benefit to workers |

## Extraction Notes

- Never describe WWF and WPPF as the same obligation, or as interchangeable percentages of the same base (income vs. profit) — they are calculated on different figures under different statutes.
- Never fold either obligation into ordinary monthly payroll tax/social-security deductions; both are typically assessed at annual-close/tax-return time, not per pay period.
- Treat any specific percentage rate or income/profit threshold as needing verification against the current Finance Act or the Acts' latest amendments, since rates have changed historically and can differ by province where administration has devolved.
- Keep both obligations distinct from EOBI old-age contributions (`eobi-employer-contributions`) and provincial social security registration (`provincial-social-security-employer-registration`), which are separate statutory regimes with different funding purposes.
