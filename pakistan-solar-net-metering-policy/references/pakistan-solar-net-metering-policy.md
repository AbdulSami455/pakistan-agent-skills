# Pakistan Solar Net-Metering Policy Reference

## Source

- NEPRA Alternative and Renewable Energy (Net-Metering) Regulations, 2015 and subsequent amendments (search NEPRA's regulations/notifications section for the current consolidated text and any amendment notifications)
- AEDB (Alternative Energy Development Board): `https://aedb.org`
- DISCO-specific net-metering SOPs and application forms (e.g., LESCO, IESCO, MEPCO, GEPCO, FESCO, PESCO, HESCO, SEPCO, QESCO) and K-Electric's net-metering procedure page for K-Electric's franchise area
- Ministry of Energy, Power Division: `https://power.gov.pk`

## Legal and Institutional Framework

- **NEPRA Act, 1997** provides NEPRA's overarching regulatory authority over electric power, under which the Net-Metering Regulations were framed as subordinate regulations specific to distributed/small-scale renewable generation.
- **Alternative and Renewable Energy (Net-Metering) Regulations, 2015** (as amended) set out: eligible technologies (solar PV predominantly; also wind, small hydro, biogas/biomass in some formulations), capacity eligibility bands (residential/commercial/industrial consumers up to a specified maximum sanctioned load-linked capacity), the interconnection agreement template, metering requirements (bi-directional/net meter), and the billing/settlement mechanism.
- **AEDB**, established under the Alternative Energy Development Board (Amendment) Ordinance and functioning under the Power Division, is the federal implementing arm for renewable-energy policy — including the Alternative and Renewable Energy Policy — technology facilitation, investor guidance, and technical standard-setting (e.g., installer/vendor guidance for solar PV), but it does not itself set the net-metering buy-back tariff; that is a NEPRA function.
- **DISCOs** (10 distribution companies under the PEPCO/CPPA-G successor structure) and K-Electric (vertically integrated in Karachi) implement net-metering at the consumer-application level under their NEPRA-approved distribution licenses, applying the national regulation through their own operational SOPs.

## Net-Metering vs. Gross-Metering

- **Net-metering**: within a billing period, the consumer's exported units are netted against imported units; the consumer is billed (or credited, sometimes with limits on cash settlement/carry-forward of excess credit) only for the net difference, generally at or near the consumer's applicable retail tariff for net consumption and at an approved buy-back rate for net export.
- **Gross-metering**: exported and imported units are metered and settled entirely separately — the consumer pays the full retail tariff for all units consumed and separately receives payment (typically at a different, often lower, rate) for all units generated/exported, regardless of self-consumption. Gross-metering has been discussed/piloted/proposed by regulators and DISCOs as an alternative to net-metering, partly to address revenue and grid-cost-recovery concerns as rooftop-solar penetration rises; the extent of formal adoption and applicable consumer categories should be verified against the current NEPRA notification (verify current status).

## Buy-Back Tariff and Recent Controversy

- The net-metering buy-back rate has historically been set with reference to the DISCO's average power purchase price or a specific NEPRA-determined rate, and has been revised more than once since 2015.
- Beginning around 2023-2025, there has been sustained public, industry, and regulatory debate in Pakistan over proposals to significantly reduce the net-metering buy-back rate and/or transition consumers to gross-metering, citing rapid growth in rooftop-solar installations and its effect on DISCO revenue recovery and grid cost allocation across remaining grid-dependent consumers. The precise rate figures, effective dates, and final regulatory outcome of these proposals change frequently and should not be stated as settled fact without checking the latest NEPRA notification (verify current figure and status).

## Extraction Notes

- Always specify whether a described mechanism is net-metering or gross-metering; do not default to net-metering without confirming.
- Attribute policy/facilitation statements to AEDB and rate/regulatory statements to NEPRA; do not merge the two institutions' roles.
- Treat any specific PKR/kWh buy-back figure, capacity threshold, or "as of [date]" status claim on the net-metering/gross-metering transition as needing current verification given the pace of regulatory revision in this area.
- Note DISCO-level variation in application turnaround and documentation requirements even though the underlying NEPRA regulation is national.
