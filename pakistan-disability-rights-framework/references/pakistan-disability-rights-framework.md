# Pakistan Disability Rights Framework Reference

## Source

- Disabled Persons (Employment and Rehabilitation) Ordinance, 1981 — the original federal statute establishing the employment quota and Council for Rehabilitation of Disabled Persons framework, applicable nationwide before the 18th Amendment's devolution of the subject to the provinces.
- Islamabad Capital Territory Persons with Disabilities Act, 2020 (ICT-specific; applicable within the federal capital territory).
- Provincial disability legislation for Punjab, Sindh, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: each province has enacted, or continues to apply an adapted/extended version of, its own disability-rights/rehabilitation framework post-devolution; exact current statute titles, years, and provisions for each province should be verified against that province's current assembly legislative record rather than assumed identical to the ICT 2020 Act or to one another.
- UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and its Optional Protocol — Pakistan is a state party (ratified); verify the exact ratification date and Optional Protocol status against the UN Treaty Collection before citing a specific date.
- National/Provincial Council(s) for Rehabilitation of Disabled Persons — statutory bodies originating under the 1981 Ordinance framework; current institutional status (whether a national council continues to function post-devolution alongside provincial councils, or has been effectively replaced by provincial structures) should be verified rather than assumed unchanged.
- Ministry of Human Rights, Government of Pakistan — the federal ministry generally responsible for coordinating Pakistan's CRPD reporting and disability-rights policy at the federal/international-obligation level post-devolution.

## Devolution and the Legislative Patchwork

- Before the 18th Constitutional Amendment (2010), disability welfare and rehabilitation were governed nationwide by the Disabled Persons (Employment and Rehabilitation) Ordinance, 1981, which established the employment quota, the registration/certification system for persons with disabilities, and the Council for Rehabilitation of Disabled Persons.
- Post-devolution, disability welfare became primarily a provincial legislative subject. The Islamabad Capital Territory, not being a province, retained federal-level legislative competence and enacted the ICT Persons with Disabilities Act, 2020, as a modernized, more rights-based replacement for the 1981 Ordinance within ICT's jurisdiction.
- Provinces have separately had to either continue applying an adapted version of the 1981 Ordinance framework, or enact their own provincial disability acts. As of this reference, do not assume any one province's statute shares the ICT 2020 Act's exact title, structure, or provisions — treat each province's current disability statute as needing independent verification.

## ICT Persons with Disabilities Act 2020 — Notable Features

- Represents a shift in framing from the 1981 Ordinance's primarily welfare/rehabilitation and employment-quota-centered approach toward a more explicitly rights-based approach influenced by CRPD principles: recognition of legal capacity, non-discrimination, accessibility (physical environment, information, communication), and inclusive education, alongside continuation of employment-quota and registration/certification mechanisms.
- Establishes (or reconstitutes, within ICT) an oversight body responsible for registration/certification of persons with disabilities and monitoring compliance with the Act's requirements, including the employment quota.
- Applicable only within the Islamabad Capital Territory; not directly binding on provincial governments or provincially regulated employers.

## Employment Quota

- The 1981 Ordinance framework originally established a statutory minimum-employment quota for persons with disabilities, commonly cited historically at around 2% of positions in covered public- and (in some formulations) private-sector establishments above a specified minimum employee-count threshold.
- Successor federal (ICT) and provincial legislation has generally continued some form of employment quota, though the exact current percentage, the minimum-employer-size threshold for coverage, and enforcement/penalty mechanisms (e.g., a compensatory levy for non-compliant employers in some formulations) vary by jurisdiction and have been subject to proposed increases in various reform and advocacy discussions.
- State any specific quota percentage or employer-size threshold with an explicit "(verify current jurisdiction-specific figure)" hedge rather than asserting one fixed number as the current universal figure, since this varies across ICT/provincial statutes and has changed over time.

## CRPD Ratification and Domestic Implementation

- Pakistan is a state party to the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD); the exact ratification date and any reservations/declarations, along with Optional Protocol status, should be verified against the UN Treaty Collection rather than cited from memory with unwarranted precision.
- CRPD ratification creates an international-law obligation for Pakistan to progressively align domestic law, policy, and institutional practice with the Convention's rights-based standards (accessibility, legal capacity, inclusive education, non-discrimination, reasonable accommodation) — ratification does not by itself make CRPD provisions automatically enforceable as domestic law; domestic effect depends on implementing legislation such as the ICT 2020 Act and provincial equivalents.
- Pakistan's periodic reporting to the CRPD Committee (state party reports and the Committee's concluding observations) is the primary international review mechanism for tracking implementation progress; specific findings/recommendations from a particular reporting cycle should be cited only from a verified, current source.
- Domestic alignment with CRPD standards is uneven and incremental across Pakistan's federal/provincial jurisdictions; avoid characterizing full CRPD alignment as achieved uniformly across the country.

## Council for Rehabilitation of Disabled Persons

- Originating under the 1981 Ordinance, the Council for Rehabilitation of Disabled Persons (at the national level, with provincial-level councils/committees in various formulations) has historically been responsible for: registration and certification of persons with disabilities (issuing disability certificates used for quota and benefit eligibility), oversight/registration of institutions providing rehabilitation and special-education services, and monitoring employer compliance with the employment quota.
- Post-devolution, the precise current institutional landscape — whether a national council continues to operate in a coordinating role alongside distinct provincial councils, or whether provincial bodies have become the primary functional authority — should be verified rather than assumed unchanged from the pre-devolution structure.
- Disability certification issued through this Council structure is commonly a prerequisite for accessing quota-based employment, certain tax concessions, and other statutory benefits tied to registered disability status.

## Extraction Notes

- Always identify the jurisdiction (ICT vs. specific province) before citing a specific disability statute's title, year, or provision.
- Hedge the employment-quota percentage and covered-employer threshold as jurisdiction- and vintage-specific rather than asserting one fixed current nationwide figure.
- Distinguish CRPD ratification (international obligation) from domestic implementing legislation (actual enforceable law) — do not conflate the two.
- Attribute registration/certification and quota-compliance oversight to the Council for Rehabilitation of Disabled Persons (or its verified current provincial equivalent), not to BISP, NADRA, or education departments.
- Note the older welfare/rehabilitation framing (1981 Ordinance) versus the newer rights-based/CRPD-aligned framing (ICT 2020 Act and comparable provincial reforms) as a real evolution, not interchangeable descriptions of the same approach.
