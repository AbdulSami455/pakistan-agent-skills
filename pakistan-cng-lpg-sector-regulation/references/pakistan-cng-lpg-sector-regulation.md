# Pakistan CNG and LPG Sector Regulation Reference

## Source

- Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA): `https://www.ogra.org.pk`
- OGRA Ordinance, 2002 (establishing OGRA's regulatory mandate over midstream/downstream petroleum, including CNG and LPG)
- Sui Northern Gas Pipelines Limited (SNGPL): `https://www.sngpl.com.pk`
- Sui Southern Gas Company (SSGC): `https://www.ssgc.com.pk`
- All Pakistan CNG Association — industry-side commentary on CNG pricing/curtailment history
- LPG Distributors Association of Pakistan / LPG industry bodies — industry-side commentary on LPG marketing-license conditions
- Ministry of Energy, Petroleum Division: `https://petroleum.gov.pk` (gas allocation policy)

## Regulatory Architecture

- OGRA regulates midstream and downstream petroleum activities under the OGRA Ordinance, 2002, including: natural gas transmission/distribution tariff-setting for SNGPL and SSGC; CNG station licensing and technical/safety standards; LPG production, storage, and marketing-company licensing; and petroleum product pricing more broadly (covered in depth by `ogra-petroleum-industry-reports`).
- CNG and LPG sit within OGRA's downstream/retail-facing regulatory functions, layered on top of (for CNG) the gas-utility supply relationship with SNGPL/SSGC, and (for LPG) an independent production/import and cylinder-distribution supply chain.

## Natural Gas (CNG) Regulatory Track

- CNG stations draw compressed natural gas from the SNGPL or SSGC distribution network under a gas-supply/connection arrangement with the relevant utility, in addition to holding an OGRA CNG station (retail outlet) license covering safety standards for compression, storage, dispensing equipment, and periodic cylinder (vehicle-tank) testing/certification requirements.
- **Pricing history**: CNG retail prices in Pakistan were, for an extended period, administratively notified by OGRA under a pricing formula historically referenced against motor-fuel (petrol) prices or a cost-of-service/gas-price-linked benchmark, with periodic controversy over the pricing gap between CNG and petrol/diesel driving fluctuating CNG demand. Policy direction has moved at various points toward greater deregulation of CNG retail pricing; the currently applicable mechanism (administered notification vs. market-determined pricing) should be verified against OGRA's current notifications rather than assumed (verify current mechanism).
- **Curtailment/load-management**: Indigenous natural gas supply in Pakistan has long been supply-constrained relative to aggregate demand across sectors (domestic, power generation, fertilizer manufacturing, general industry, and CNG). Government gas-allocation policy has historically ranked CNG relatively low in the priority order, particularly in winter months when domestic heating demand surges — resulting in periodic CNG station closures or restricted supply days/hours ("CNG load-shedding") set by government/OGRA notification, sometimes varying by region (e.g., differential treatment between provinces/gas-utility service areas). Current curtailment schedules and the specific priority order in force should be verified against current government/OGRA notifications rather than treated as a fixed rule (verify current schedule).
- CNG sector size and station count in Pakistan grew rapidly in the 2000s (Pakistan was for a period among the world's largest CNG-vehicle markets by station count) before contracting due to sustained gas curtailment and price competitiveness pressure from LPG/petrol substitution — treat specific station-count or market-size figures as needing current verification rather than citing outdated peak-era numbers as current.

## LPG Regulatory Track

- LPG in Pakistan is sourced both domestically (as a by-product of gas-processing plants and oil refineries) and via import (particularly to cover demand in areas without natural gas pipeline coverage), then distributed largely via cylinder retail (and to a lesser extent bulk/auto-gas) rather than through the SNGPL/SSGC piped network.
- OGRA licenses LPG activity across distinct stages: (a) LPG production/extraction licensing for gas-processing facilities; (b) LPG marketing company (LPGMC) licensing covering storage/bottling-plant operation and cylinder distribution/marketing; (c) potentially separate licensing considerations for LPG import/terminal operations. Marketing companies must generally meet OGRA-set minimum storage-capacity and safety-infrastructure requirements to hold/retain a marketing license.
- LPG cylinder pricing in Pakistan has generally been more market-determined (linked to international LPG/propane-butane benchmark prices and PKR exchange-rate movements) than the historically administered CNG pricing approach, though OGRA has at times issued indicative/maximum retail price notifications for LPG cylinders — the current pricing approach should be verified rather than assumed (verify current mechanism).
- LPG is particularly significant as a cooking/heating fuel substitute in areas of Pakistan (especially in parts of Balochistan, KP, and rural/off-network areas) without access to the SNGPL/SSGC piped natural gas network, making LPG marketing-license regulation and pricing a distinct policy concern from urban piped-gas/CNG issues.

## Extraction Notes

- Always specify CNG or LPG explicitly; do not use "gas sector" generically when the source distinguishes the two.
- For CNG, separate the station-licensing/safety function (OGRA), the supply/allocation function (SNGPL/SSGC plus government gas-allocation policy), and the pricing-mechanism function (OGRA notification history) as three distinct regulatory threads.
- For LPG, separate production/import licensing from marketing/distribution licensing, and note LPG's largely off-pipeline, import-exposed supply chain.
- Hedge all specific current price figures, curtailment schedules, and station/market-size statistics as needing verification given the sector's volatile regulatory and market history.
