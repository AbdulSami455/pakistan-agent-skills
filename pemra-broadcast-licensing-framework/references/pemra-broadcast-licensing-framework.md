# PEMRA Broadcast Licensing Framework Reference

## Source

- PEMRA Ordinance, 2002 (as amended) — text available via PEMRA's laws/ordinances section: `https://www.pemra.gov.pk`

## Legal Basis

The **Pakistan Electronic Media Regulatory Authority Ordinance, 2002** established PEMRA as the regulator for private electronic media (broadcast television, FM radio, cable TV distribution, and — through later amendments and policy extensions — satellite/DTH distribution and web/OTT content). The Ordinance has been amended over time to expand PEMRA's scope (for example, extending regulatory reach toward newer distribution technologies and content platforms); the current, amended text should be consulted for the up-to-date scope rather than relying on the Ordinance's original 2002 form alone.

## License Category Eligibility, Term, and Fee Mechanics

PEMRA's licensing regulations (issued under the Ordinance) set category-specific conditions that go beyond simply naming a category:

- **Ownership/eligibility conditions**: licensing regulations impose eligibility requirements on applicants (such as being a company incorporated in Pakistan for broadcast licenses, and conditions/limits relevant to foreign ownership or cross-media ownership concentration within a category). Exact current ownership-cap percentages and eligibility thresholds should be verified against PEMRA's current licensing regulations, as these are administratively defined and subject to revision.
- **License term and renewal**: broadcast and distribution licenses are issued for a fixed term (commonly multi-year, category-dependent) and are subject to a renewal process before expiry; a license is not perpetual, and continued operation requires timely renewal under PEMRA's process.
- **Competitive/open bidding**: where the number of qualified applicants for a given license category and geographic/coverage slot exceeds the licenses available, PEMRA licensing regulations provide for open, competitive bidding among eligible applicants rather than first-come allocation.
- **Fee structure**: license fees (application fee, annual/periodic license fee) are category- and often coverage-area-dependent (e.g., a national satellite TV license fee structure differs from a regional/provincial-scope TV license or an FM radio license for a specific city). Specific current fee amounts should be verified against PEMRA's current fee schedule rather than treated as fixed.

## Landing Rights Authorization

"Landing rights" is a distinct authorization PEMRA grants allowing a **foreign-origin satellite TV channel** to be legally retransmitted/distributed within Pakistan through licensed local distributors (cable operators, DTH operators, or IPTV where applicable). Key distinctions:

- The foreign channel itself is not issued a Pakistani broadcast-origination license (it is not a PEMRA-licensed Pakistani broadcaster); landing rights instead clear that specific foreign channel for lawful local carriage/retransmission by PEMRA-licensed Pakistani distribution operators.
- Distributors (cable/DTH operators) may only carry foreign channels that hold valid landing rights clearance; carrying a foreign channel without landing rights exposes the distributor (not the foreign channel, which is outside PEMRA's direct licensing jurisdiction) to enforcement action.
- Landing rights approval is a separate administrative process from domestic license issuance, with its own application and periodic renewal/review requirements.

## Cable TV and DTH Distribution Licensing

- **Cable TV distribution license**: authorizes a licensee to operate a cable television distribution system (headend plus physical/coaxial or hybrid distribution network) within a defined franchise/coverage area, carrying a mix of domestic PEMRA-licensed channels and landing-rights-cleared foreign channels.
- **DTH (Direct-to-Home) distribution license**: authorizes satellite-based direct-to-home distribution of a channel bouquet to subscriber dish/set-top-box installations, a technologically distinct distribution method from cable's physical network, licensed as its own category with separate technical and coverage conditions.
- These are distribution-layer licenses, distinct from a content-origination broadcast license (satellite TV or FM radio channel license); a single corporate group may hold licenses in multiple categories, but each is a separate authorization with its own conditions.

## Statutory Penalty/Enforcement Escalation Sequence

The PEMRA Ordinance and associated Code of Conduct rules establish an escalation framework for licensing or content violations, generally following this sequence (procedural detail and exact thresholds should be confirmed against the current Ordinance/rules text):

1. **Notice / show-cause notice**: PEMRA issues a notice identifying the alleged violation and affording the licensee an opportunity to respond before further action — a statutory procedural safeguard rather than an optional courtesy step.
2. **Monetary fine**: for confirmed violations not warranting immediate suspension, PEMRA may impose a monetary penalty within limits set by the Ordinance/rules.
3. **Suspension of license/transmission**: for more serious or repeated violations, PEMRA may suspend the license or direct suspension of transmission for a specified period, typically following a hearing before the Authority (or a Council of Complaints mechanism for certain content-related grievances).
4. **Revocation/cancellation**: the most severe step, involving formal cancellation of the license, reserved for serious, repeated, or uncured violations and generally requiring the fullest procedural process (show-cause, hearing, and a formal Authority decision) under the Ordinance.

PEMRA also operates **Councils of Complaints** at regional levels as part of the content-grievance process, which can receive and process public complaints about broadcast content as an input into the enforcement pathway, distinct from PEMRA's own direct monitoring-driven enforcement actions.
