# Pakistan Mental Health and Substance Abuse Reference

## Source

- Mental Health Ordinance, 2001 (federal; enacted before the 18th Amendment devolved health legislation to the provinces in 2010).
- Sindh Mental Health Act, 2013 (Sindh's provincial mental health legislation, enacted post-devolution).
- Punjab, KP, and Balochistan provincial mental health legislation status: each province has considered or moved toward its own mental health act in the post-devolution period; the current in-force statute and exact title/year for each of these three provinces should be verified against the relevant provincial assembly's current legislative record rather than assumed, as this is an area with ongoing/uneven provincial legislative activity.
- Control of Narcotics Substances Act, 1997 (CNSA), as amended.
- Anti-Narcotics Force (ANF) official website: `https://anf.gov.pk` (verify current domain before citing as a live link).
- Ministry of Narcotics Control, Government of Pakistan — the federal ministry under which ANF operates.
- UNODC Pakistan country programme materials, referenced for international counter-narcotics coordination context (background source, not a substitute for Pakistan's own statutory text).
- Pakistan's National Drug Control Policy / National Anti-Narcotics Policy documents (periodically issued by the Ministry of Narcotics Control) — verify the current policy document's title and year before citing specific targets or strategic pillars from it.

## Mental Health Ordinance 2001 — Structure

- Established Mental Health Authorities (federal and, where adopted, provincial-level boards) intended to oversee psychiatric facility licensing, monitor patient welfare, and review involuntary admissions.
- Defined admission categories: informal/voluntary admission (a person consents to admission and treatment) and involuntary/compulsory admission (admission without the patient's consent, subject to medical certification requirements and statutory safeguards).
- Involuntary admission safeguards generally include: certification by a specified number/category of qualified medical practitioners, a time-limited initial admission order, periodic medical review for continued detention, and a mechanism for the patient (or a representative) to apply for discharge or challenge continued detention before the relevant Mental Health Authority/Board or a court.
- The Ordinance also addressed management of the property of persons found to lack capacity, and offences relating to ill-treatment or exploitation of psychiatric patients.
- Implementation capacity — i.e., whether Mental Health Authorities/Boards were actually constituted and functioning, and whether psychiatric facilities were actually licensed and monitored as intended — has been a recurring concern raised in secondary/NGO and academic commentary; treat implementation-gap claims as a softer-sourced, generally-acknowledged policy critique distinct from the statutory text itself, and avoid citing specific compliance statistics without a current source.

## Post-18th Amendment Provincial Devolution

- The 18th Constitutional Amendment (2010) moved health (including mental health) from the Concurrent Legislative List to a provincial subject, meaning provinces are now the primary legislative authority for mental health law.
- Sindh enacted the Sindh Mental Health Act, 2013, following broadly the same structural template as the 2001 Ordinance (licensing/oversight authority, admission categories, review safeguards), adapted to Sindh's provincial institutional structure.
- For Punjab, KP, and Balochistan, the precise current in-force mental health statute (whether each has enacted its own act, continues to apply the 2001 Ordinance by adaptation/extension, or has a bill pending) should be verified against current provincial legislative records rather than assumed uniform; do not assert a specific title/year for these three provinces without that verification.

## Control of Narcotics Substances Act 1997 — Structure

- The CNSA 1997 is the principal federal statute criminalizing the production, manufacture, possession, trafficking, and financing of offences involving narcotic drugs, psychotropic substances, and controlled/precursor chemicals.
- The Act's schedules classify substances (narcotic drugs, psychotropic substances, controlled substances/precursor chemicals) and set out quantity thresholds that distinguish categories of offence severity (e.g., a small quantity for personal use/simple possession versus larger, trafficking-level quantities), with penalties scaling accordingly, including the most severe penalty tiers (up to life imprisonment or, in the statute's provisions for large-scale trafficking, capital punishment exposure in its original/amended form) reserved for large-quantity trafficking and related offences — specific current threshold quantities (e.g., grams/kilograms distinguishing offence tiers) and penalty tiers should be flagged "(verify current schedule/amendment)" rather than stated with invented precision, since schedules and penalty provisions have been amended.
- The CNSA establishes Special Courts for the trial of narcotics offences, with their own procedural framework layered on top of (and in some respects distinct from) general CrPC criminal procedure.
- The Act also addresses asset forfeiture/confiscation of property connected to narcotics offences and controls over precursor chemicals used in drug manufacture.

## Anti-Narcotics Force (ANF)

- ANF is the federal law-enforcement agency principally responsible for narcotics-control enforcement under the CNSA 1997, operating under the Ministry of Narcotics Control.
- Core mandate: interdiction of narcotics trafficking (including at international borders/ports, given Pakistan's proximity to Afghan opiate-production and trafficking routes historically flagged in UNODC reporting), investigation of narcotics offences, precursor-chemical control, and international counter-narcotics coordination.
- ANF also operates (or coordinates with) treatment/rehabilitation facilities as part of a demand-reduction function that sits alongside its core enforcement mandate — the balance and current scale of ANF's own treatment-facility operations versus other government/NGO providers should be verified rather than assumed.
- ANF is a distinct agency from provincial police narcotics units, from FIA, and from NCCIA; narcotics offences investigated by ANF proceed through CNSA Special Courts, not through NCCIA's PECA-specific process or an ordinary police-station FIR/challan process in the same form (though overlapping general criminal-procedure concepts, such as arrest safeguards, remain relevant as a backdrop).

## Treatment, Rehabilitation, and Demand-Reduction Framework

- Pakistan's narcotics policy generally references the international drug-policy framing of supply reduction (enforcement/interdiction), demand reduction (prevention, treatment, rehabilitation), and, to a lesser and more contested extent, harm reduction.
- Treatment/rehabilitation services are delivered through a mix of federal/provincial government-run facilities, ANF-affiliated rehabilitation centers, and NGO/private treatment providers; the current national drug-control policy document (title, year, and specific strategic targets) should be verified before citing specific policy commitments or funding figures.
- Drug-demand-reduction efforts in Pakistan are frequently discussed in relation to specific high-prevalence-substance concerns (e.g., historically, opiates/heroin, and more recently synthetic/psychotropic substances and precursor-chemical diversion) — cite specific prevalence statistics only from a current, named source rather than from memory.

## Extraction Notes

- Always distinguish whether a question concerns mental health (admission/patient-rights framework) or substance abuse/narcotics (ANF/CNSA enforcement) before answering.
- For mental health questions, state explicitly whether the answer describes the historical federal 2001 Ordinance or a specific province's current statute (confirmed only for Sindh's 2013 Act among the four provinces as of this reference; hedge for Punjab, KP, Balochistan).
- For narcotics questions, keep ANF's enforcement/interdiction role distinct from treatment/rehabilitation policy, and from general CrPC criminal procedure and other agencies' mandates (FIA, NCCIA, provincial police).
- Flag specific quantity thresholds, penalty tiers, and current policy-document titles/years as "(verify)" rather than asserting precise unconfirmed figures.
