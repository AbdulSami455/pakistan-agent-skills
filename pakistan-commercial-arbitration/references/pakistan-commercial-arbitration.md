# Pakistan Commercial Arbitration Reference

## Source

- Arbitration Act, 1940 (Pakistan) — governing statute for domestic arbitration: available via Pakistan Code / provincial law department portals.
- Recognition and Enforcement (Arbitration Agreements and Foreign Arbitral Awards) Act, 2011 (REA 2011) — implements the New York Convention in Pakistan: available via Pakistan Code / National Assembly publications.
- New York Convention (Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards, 1958) — Pakistan acceded 2005: `https://www.newyorkconvention.org`
- ICSID Case No. ARB/12/1, Tethyan Copper Company Pty Limited v. Islamic Republic of Pakistan: `https://icsid.worldbank.org`
- Draft Arbitration Bill/Act 2024 (UNCITRAL Model Law-based reform proposal) — status should be verified against Parliament's own record via `parliament-legislative-tracker`.

## Governing Statutes by Situation

| Situation | Governing Law |
|---|---|
| Domestic, Pakistan-seated arbitration | Arbitration Act, 1940 |
| Recognition/enforcement of a foreign arbitral award or foreign arbitration agreement | Recognition and Enforcement Act, 2011 (REA 2011) / New York Convention |
| Investment-treaty dispute (foreign investor vs. Pakistan as respondent state) | Relevant bilateral investment treaty + ICSID Convention or UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules |

## Arbitration Act, 1940 (Domestic)

A pre-independence, colonial-era statute still governing domestic arbitration. It does not distinguish domestic from international arbitration in its drafting and is widely criticized for permitting extensive court intervention at multiple stages (reference to arbitration, during the arbitral process, and at the award-challenge stage). It provides for three broad categories: arbitration without court intervention, arbitration through court process where no suit is pending, and arbitration in a pending suit through court.

## Recognition and Enforcement Act, 2011 (Foreign Awards)

- Pakistan signed the New York Convention in 1958 and ratified it in 2005, implementing it domestically first via a 2005 Ordinance (repeatedly re-promulgated) and finally via REA 2011, an Act of Parliament.
- REA 2011 gives the High Courts jurisdiction to recognize and enforce foreign arbitral awards, and to refer parties to arbitration under a valid arbitration agreement, largely treating the award as if it were a High Court order.
- Refusal-of-enforcement grounds are limited to New York Convention Article V categories: invalid arbitration agreement, due-process defects, award beyond the scope of submission, tribunal composition/procedure not per the parties' agreement, award not yet binding or set aside at the seat, non-arbitrability of the subject matter, or public-policy conflict. Enforcement is not open to full merits review.

## Draft Arbitration Bill/Act 2024 (Pending Reform)

A draft Arbitration Act modeled substantially on the UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration has been circulated and discussed (including presentation to the Federal Law Minister in 2024) as a proposed replacement for the 1940 Act. As of the most recently verifiable reporting, it remains a draft/bill, not enacted law. Any claim that it is already in force should be treated as unverified and checked against `parliament-legislative-tracker` or the official Gazette.

## Investment-Treaty (ICSID) Arbitration

Pakistan's most prominent investment-treaty case is Tethyan Copper Company Pty Limited v. Islamic Republic of Pakistan (ICSID Case No. ARB/12/1), arising from the Reko Diq mining-lease dispute in Balochistan, brought under the Australia-Pakistan bilateral investment treaty. The tribunal found Pakistan liable for treaty breaches including fair-and-equitable-treatment violations and unlawful expropriation, with a very large damages award later subject to settlement negotiations. Treat any specific dollar figure, settlement term, or case-status detail as requiring verification against current reporting, since this dispute has evolved over years through post-award settlement discussions.

## Practice Notes

- Pakistani commercial arbitration remains predominantly ad hoc rather than institutional; use of institutional rules (ICC, LCIA, SIAC, or a domestic institution) exists but is not the default mode.
- Courts' historical willingness to intervene in the arbitral process under the 1940 Act has been cited by practitioners as limiting confidence in arbitration as a genuinely faster alternative to litigation for Pakistan-seated matters.

## Extraction Notes

- Never conflate the 1940 Act (domestic arbitration procedure) with REA 2011 (foreign award/agreement recognition and enforcement) — they operate in separate lanes.
- Never state the Draft Arbitration Bill/Act 2024 is currently in force without fresh verification.
- Never conflate a private commercial arbitration dispute with an investment-treaty (ICSID) dispute — different legal regime, different forum, different remedies, even though both are "arbitration involving Pakistan."
- Treat any ICSID award figure or settlement detail as needing verification against current reporting rather than a fixed historical figure.
