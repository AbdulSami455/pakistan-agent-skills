## Constitutional and Institutional Framework

- Under Pakistan's Constitution, mines and minerals (excluding oil, natural gas, and nuclear minerals, which remain federal subjects) fall within provincial legislative and executive competence, reinforced by the 18th Constitutional Amendment (2010) devolution of subjects previously on the Concurrent List.
- Each province operates its own mining concession rules setting out exploration license, mining lease, and quarry lease procedures alongside fiscal schedules (royalty, dead-rent/rent, surface rent) — the rules are broadly similar in structure across provinces (patterned on a common colonial-era/Pakistan Mining Concession Rules lineage) but differ in specific rates, mineral classifications, and administrative detail.
- The federal government's role is largely policy-coordination (National Mineral Policy), foreign-investment facilitation for large strategic projects, and any federally-legislated special-purpose frameworks for individual projects of national significance (as with Reko Diq).

## Standard Fiscal Instruments in Provincial Mining Rules

- **Royalty**: a charge on the mineral extracted, typically set per province per mineral category (either as a fixed rate per tonne/unit or an ad valorem percentage of value), payable to the provincial government; schedules are periodically revised and vary significantly by mineral (e.g., coal, copper, gold, industrial minerals, dimension stone, gemstones each often have distinct royalty schedules).
- **Dead-rent (or "rent")**: a fixed annual charge on the leased area irrespective of whether production occurs, intended to discourage speculative land-holding; typically structured so that royalty paid in a year is credited against (or the higher of royalty/dead-rent is payable), though the precise crediting mechanism varies by province's rules.
- **Surface rent**: compensation payable (often to the landowner, sometimes routed through provincial government) for use/occupation of surface land for mining operations, separate from subsurface mineral rights.
- **Other provincial charges**: some provinces levy additional cesses, excise duties on minerals transported out of the province, or infrastructure/development cesses layered on top of royalty.

## Reko Diq Fiscal Structure (High-Level, Verify Specifics)

- The Reko Diq copper-gold deposit in Chagai district, Balochistan, was the subject of a long-running international arbitration dispute (ICSID) following the 2011 denial of a mining lease to Tethyan Copper Company; this was resolved through a 2022 reconstitution agreement.
- Under the 2022 restructuring, the project ownership was reorganized to include Barrick Gold (as operator, holding a substantial equity stake), with the remaining equity split among Pakistani state-owned/provincial entities — reportedly including the Government of Balochistan and federal state-owned enterprises — alongside dedicated enabling legislation and fiscal/regulatory stabilization arrangements intended to give the project a defined, negotiated fiscal regime (royalty and profit-sharing terms) distinct from a province's generic mineral rules schedule.

## National Mineral Policy

- Pakistan's National Mineral Policy sets federal-level objectives for the minerals sector — attracting domestic and foreign investment, streamlining and harmonizing provincial licensing/fiscal frameworks, improving geological data availability, and positioning Pakistan's fiscal terms competitively against regional mining jurisdictions — but implementation of royalty/rent schedules remains a provincial rule-making function.
- Policy revisions have periodically recommended royalty-rate rationalization and simplified lease-conversion processes; whether a specific recommendation has been adopted into a province's binding mineral rules should be checked against that province's current rules rather than assumed from the policy document alone.

- Always name the specific province whose mineral rules a royalty or rent figure is drawn from; do not present a rate as national.
- Keep royalty, dead-rent, and surface rent conceptually and numerically separate.

- Treat the National Mineral Policy as aspirational/coordinating, not as itself a binding rate schedule.
